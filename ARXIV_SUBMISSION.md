## Title

LLMs Read the Room Better Than Humans

## Authors

Victor Lavrenko

PeaceTech VC, Jerusalem, Israel  
Email: victor@peacetech.vc  

## Abstract

Large language models are often criticized for producing generic “AI slop.” Yet when asked which message people are more likely to share, they perform remarkably well. On 1,142 historical same-author/same-link Twitter pairs, six LLMs outperform individual humans; the best reaches 79.7% accuracy, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) of 77.3–81.9, versus 61.3% for individuals and 73.0% for a 39-person majority. A sensitivity model places the text-only ceiling near 85%, suggesting that strong models capture most of the predictable signal in text. We then tested the comparison with contemporary humans in Beat the Robot: players lost 69 of 70 matches. To test whether memorization of the old benchmark explains the result, we repeated the same prediction task on fresh 2026 Bluesky data; its confidence interval includes the original benchmark estimate. Therefore, LLMs already anticipate what audiences will share remarkably well, motivating generation systems that create alternatives, self-evaluate audience response, and revise toward stronger outputs.
